# User Model

Use this file when the user asks for personalisation, when a missing preference materially changes the output, or when updating the user's portable model.

## No default identity assumptions

Treat these as configurable fields:
- pronouns / gender;
- sexuality / orientation;
- relationship orientation;
- current relationship status or structure;
- target partner gender(s) or kinds of people;
- ENM/poly relevance;
- location and practical radius;
- disclosure needs;
- seriousness, pace, and escalation preferences;
- comfort with different relationship structures;
- desired amount of flirtation, edge, directness, and vulnerability.

Use only supplied facts, artifact evidence, or clearly labelled provisional inferences.

## Fast-start interview

Ask 6-10 questions only. Prioritise what changes live decisions:
1. Who or what kinds of people are you trying to date right now?
2. What relationship outcome are you genuinely seeking?
3. What relationship styles or structures are compatible for you?
4. What should this copilot never assume about you?
5. What kind of flirting feels most like you?
6. What kinds of people are attractive but repeatedly poor-fit?
7. What are your hard no's?
8. What counts as a promising signal?
9. What is your preferred pace from chat to meeting?
10. What location/practical constraints matter?

## Deep interview themes

Use only when requested: relationship goals, structure preferences, attraction patterns, blind spots, values, lifestyle, ambition, conflict style, flirt style, voice, boundaries, location, and outcome history.

## Updating rules

When new information appears, update the model as:
```text
Update:
Evidence:
Confidence:
Implication for future reads/messages:
```

Do not turn one outcome into a universal rule. Treat it as evidence until repeated.

## State consistency

When the user corrects a preference, update all affected fields, not only the immediately mentioned one. A voice correction may affect Voiceprint, flirt intensity, overhit risk, preferred examples, and future draft style. A relationship-goal correction may affect target signals, pacing, challenge handling, and exit criteria.

Treat user corrections as state updates, not one-off comments.
