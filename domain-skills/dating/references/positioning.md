# Positioning

The user's market identity. Positioning informs profile copy, opener choice, charge calibration, vulnerability ceiling, qualification framing, and conversion timing. It sits alongside `user-model.md` and `voiceprint.md` as a portable asset; together they define who the user is, what he sounds like, and what he offers in this market.

## Market diagnosis

The dating market for women with options does not lack male desire. It lacks calibrated desire. The commodity surplus is:

- attention;
- compliments;
- sexual want;
- vague "let's see where it goes" energy;
- low-effort flirting;
- fake confidence;
- over-eagerness.

A woman with options can sort through twenty of these in an evening. The scarce signal is desire that is specific, restrained, reciprocal, and worth acting on. Positioning targets that scarcity.

## What stands out is not wanting her

Wanting her is the commodity. The differentiating combination is:

- I see you clearly.
- I am sexually alive but not needy.
- I am warm but selective.
- I can create charge without pressure.
- I can lead without controlling.
- I know what I want, and I am checking whether you actually fit.

This combination is rare; profile copy and messages should reinforce it cumulatively, not by stating it.

## Core positioning statement

The user's offer reduces to:

> Erotic charge without chaos. Depth without heaviness. Directness without pressure. Standards without bitterness.

Or in narrative form:

> I am not another man trying to consume you. I am a man who can create chemistry, clarity, and momentum without making you manage me.

Drafts that contradict either statement should be rewritten regardless of how clever the surface is.

## Scarcity stack

Five qualities that make the user genuinely uncommon. Every message, every profile line, every charged move should reinforce at least one. None alone is positioning; the combination is.

### 1. Erotic intelligence

Knowing how to build tension, when to escalate, when to hold back, how to make desire feel mutual rather than dumped. Operationally this is the full charge framework in `references/message-kernel.md` Erotic charge: ladder discipline, four levers, Notice→tease→reveal→imply→invite, vulnerability under restraint.

Cheap-substitute failure: crude desire, fake dominance, performed sexual confidence (see `references/boundaries.md` Erotic-charge anti-patterns).

### 2. Selective warmth

Warmth without selectivity reads as approval-seeking; selectivity without warmth reads as bitterness. The combination — warm because the user likes people, but does not hand attention to anyone simply because they are attractive — is a distinct lever.

Profile and message must transmit both. Saying *"You're cute, but I'm more curious about whether there's actual mischief and emotional intelligence under the photos"* combines warm recognition with explicit selectivity in one line. See `references/voiceprint.md` Frame stance.

### 3. Low-chaos directness

Especially material in ENM/poly contexts where the market is full of vague, avoidant, or under-communicating self-described "open" people. The user's structure is honest, matter-of-fact, and free of the chaos-as-freedom failure mode.

Working register: *"Poly/ENM, allergic to chaos dressed up as freedom. I like things playful, honest, and emotionally adult."* See `references/boundaries.md` ENM/poly disclosure anti-patterns for the failure forms.

### 4. A felt life

Non-neediness that is real, not performed. The user has taste, friends, direction, and ongoing desire — life that exists independently of any single thread. This is the inverse of performed unavailability; the absence of need shows up in specifics, not in scarcity tactics.

Diagnostic: a draft that would only make sense if the user had no other matches, no plans this week, and nothing else going on is leaking neediness. A draft that fits naturally inside a real life is positioning correctly. Performed unavailability (late replies as tactic, contrived "busy" framing) fails this test from the other direction — see `references/boundaries.md` Performed unavailability.

### 5. Good logistics

Underrated. A woman can be attracted and still not meet if logistics feel annoying, vague, risky, or effortful. The conversion move must make the next step feel clean: specific time options, low-effort venue, no-pressure framing.

Working: *"Thursday or Sunday, somewhere with eye contact and decent drinks?"* Anti: *"We should hang sometime if you're free."* See `references/templates.md` Date transition and `references/message-kernel.md` Conversion move.

## What she is screening for

Beyond surface attraction, she is asking implicit questions throughout the funnel. Use these as a silent diagnostic when reading the thread — does the user's last few moves answer these positively?

- Will this be worth the effort?
- Will he be weird in a bad way?
- Will he understand pacing?
- Will he make me feel desired or consumed?
- Will I have to manage his emotions?
- Will he be clear?
- Will he be fun?
- Will he be safe without being boring?
- Will he be direct without being pushy?

If the recent exchange does not answer at least three of these in the user's favour, the next move should focus on doing so. This is a positioning-side check that complements the reciprocal-investment ladder in `references/profile-conversation-read.md` and the chemistry check in `references/message-kernel.md`.

## What makes her run to meet

She becomes eager when she feels five things at once:

1. He gets something specific about me.
2. He is attracted, but not needy.
3. He is fun to interact with.
4. He has standards I want to meet.
5. Meeting him feels exciting, safe, and easy.

Most threads only land one or two. Positioning that compounds across the thread lands all five before the conversion move. If the conversion move is being delayed despite warm signals, check which of the five are not yet landed — usually 1 (specific recognition), 4 (visible standard), or 5 (clean logistics).

## Chase mechanism

Women do not chase because asked. They chase when the frame creates: curiosity, emotional reward, charge, uncertainty without insecurity, a standard she wants to meet, and a clear next step.

Chase signals (overlap with the reciprocal-investment ladder in `references/profile-conversation-read.md`):

- she asks questions back;
- she tries to impress;
- she gives longer answers;
- she teases the user;
- she reopens the thread after a pause;
- she suggests availability;
- she qualifies herself against the user's stated standard;
- she follows the user's frame;
- she makes meeting easier.

When these appear, reward the investment per `references/message-kernel.md` Reward investment, and re-evaluate `lead_tier` per `references/scoring.md` — chase behaviour usually upgrades a thread.

## The felt experience

Positioning is not a line. It is what the thread feels like to her, cumulatively:

> Talking to him feels different. He is attracted but not desperate. He is playful but not unserious. He is direct but not pushy. He is emotionally aware but not heavy. He is selective, and I want to see if I qualify.

Drafts and profile copy succeed when they produce this felt experience across the thread, not when any single line is clever. Single-line cleverness without the felt experience reads as performance; the felt experience without any clever lines still works.
