# Message Kernel

Use this file for openers, replies, rewrites, clarifications, meeting transitions, revive-once messages, clean exits, and tone changes. This is the main generation engine.

## Central formula

```text
Message = Hook + State + Reveal + Tension + Return
```

- **Hook**: respond to the actual artifact.
- **State**: transmit an emotional state, not just information.
- **Reveal**: show something real about the user.
- **Tension**: add attraction, contrast, playful challenge, direct interest, or romantic subtext.
- **Return**: leave an easy ball to hit back.

Shorter version:
```text
Respond + Reveal + Flirt + Invite
```

## State palette

Choose one:
- amused curiosity;
- warm mischief;
- grounded directness;
- soft intrigue;
- playful suspicion;
- romantic clarity;
- decisive invitation;
- calm boundary.

## Tension moves

Use one when appropriate:
- playful hypothesis;
- warm challenge;
- specific compliment;
- playful standard;
- callback;
- romantic subtext;
- compatibility invitation;
- date-shaped suggestion;
- warmth with restraint;
- light disqualification about fit or taste.

## Social dynamics primitives

Use only the clean form:
- interested without auditioning;
- frame clarity;
- playful tension;
- compatibility invitation;
- playful standards;
- warmth with restraint;
- reciprocal investment;
- embodied value through specifics;
- next smallest escalation;
- open loop.

## Chemistry check

A useful message usually contains at least three of five chemistry ingredients:
- **Recognition**: it notices something specific in the artifact.
- **Warmth**: it feels human and inviting.
- **Tension**: it contains contrast, play, flirtation, or romantic subtext.
- **Revelation**: it shows something real about the user.
- **Momentum**: it gives the exchange somewhere to go.

If a draft is accurate but flat, add recognition or tension. If it is charged but thin, add warmth or revelation. If it is pleasant but stagnant, add momentum.

## Escalation ladder

Move up only one level unless the artifact clearly invites more.

0. functional: answer the literal message.
1. warm specificity: notice something concrete.
2. playful frame: add banter, callback, or playful hypothesis.
3. romantic subtext: signal attraction indirectly.
4. direct interest: state interest clearly but lightly.
5. date transition: propose a real-world test.

If reciprocal investment is low, do not climb the ladder; either improve signal once or let the thread go.

## Protective skepticism pattern

When the other person shows concern about the user's relationship style, intent, lifestyle, availability, or seriousness:
1. Acknowledge why the concern could be reasonable.
2. State the user's standard plainly.
3. Distinguish the user's actual intent from the feared pattern.
4. Invite the other person's standard or perspective.

Keep it grounded and concise. Concern deserves clarity; playful skepticism may also take a warmer edge.

## Frame check

Before drafting, ask:
- What frame did the other person offer?
- Should the user accept, play with, reframe, or decline it?
- Does the reply preserve self-possession?
- Does it express interest without overinvesting beyond the evidence?

## Warm-edge pass

Before finalising, check:
- warm enough to feel human?
- edged enough to avoid blandness?
- self-respecting without contempt?
- desire without entitlement?
- tension without coercion?
- grounded rather than performative?

## Anti-blandness pass

Transform:
- generic -> specific;
- polite -> alive;
- question chain -> statement + invitation;
- anxious -> grounded;
- over-explained -> compressed;
- platonic -> romantically intentional;
- clever-only -> warm and connected;
- over-edged -> kind and playable.

## Flirt intensity dial

0. neutral / informational
1. warm
2. playful
3. flirty
4. charged
5. direct desire

Default to level 1-3 unless the other person has clearly reciprocated more.

## Attractor score

Before final output, check silently or briefly when useful:
- sendability;
- specificity;
- voice match;
- romantic charge;
- mutual agency;
- signal yield;
- compression.

## Signal test

Every message should reveal something useful. Before finalising, identify what the reply is testing for: reciprocal investment, play, seriousness, relationship-style fit, attraction, availability, logistics, or mismatch.

A good message is not merely attractive; it creates a clean next signal. The `Watch` line should name that signal.

## Qualification as flirtation

Compatibility tests should not feel like an interview. Bad: "What are you looking for?" / "What do you do?" / "Are you emotionally mature?" These are dead questions; they extract data without creating charge.

Replace each with a charged form that tests the same thing:

| Testing for | Dead form | Charged form |
|---|---|---|
| Seriousness | "What are you looking for?" | "What kind of connection actually keeps your attention once the novelty wears off?" |
| Ambition | "What do you do?" | "What are you building right now that you're quietly proud of?" |
| Playfulness | "Are you fun?" | "What's your most defensible red flag?" |
| Emotional directness | "Are you emotionally mature?" | "When you actually like someone, are you direct about it or do you make them complete a small emotional obstacle course first?" |
| ENM/poly fit (when relevant) | "What do you think about non-monogamy?" | "I'm allergic to chaos dressed up as freedom. What does good non-monogamy look like to you?" |

The charged form gives her a frame to step into; people invest more when there is something to play with.

## Standards in frame

Plant the user's standard inside the message so she can self-qualify into it. This is the inverse of asking her to prove fit; it lets her show fit voluntarily.

Pattern: state preference as observation, not demand. She can either step in or fall out.

- "I tend to like women who are playful but not avoidant. Dangerous distinction."
- "I'm usually most drawn to women who have both softness and a spine."
- "I like chemistry, but I'm not really built for connections that are all spark and no substance."

The good response is her stepping in: "I'm playful but actually pretty direct when I like someone." That is qualification working. A non-response or deflection is signal too.

Do not overuse — once or twice across an early thread is plenty. Repeated standards-in-frame becomes lecturing.

## Reward investment

When she invests (specific reveal, real answer, plays back, asks a real question), reward it within one message. Two-step pattern:

1. Acknowledge the specific thing — not "thanks for sharing" but a one-line read of what she revealed.
2. Either deepen ("what brings out the [specific thing]?") or escalate ("this is probably better tested over a drink").

Rewarding investment trains the dynamic. Withholding warmth after a real reveal flattens the thread.

## Erotic charge

**Erotic charge is desire under restraint.** It is the felt current of mutual attraction, possibility, and anticipation that builds across a thread when both sides are participating. It is distinct from sexualisation — sexualisation states wanting; charge creates the shared frame in which wanting becomes legible to both people without being declared.

The formula:

```text
safety + specificity + desire + restraint + reciprocity = erotic charge
```

Each element matters. Remove safety and the thread becomes pressure. Remove specificity and it becomes generic. Remove desire and it becomes platonic. Remove restraint and it becomes crude. Remove reciprocity and it becomes broadcasting.

### Charge ladder

Seven levels. Move up only when the previous level is reciprocated. Skipping levels is the most common failure.

| Level | Register | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Warm recognition | Make her feel specifically noticed. | "There's a very specific kind of mischief in your profile. I'm not sure whether to trust it yet." |
| 2. Playful tension | Introduce small friction; give her something to push against. | "I like that answer, but I'm not ready to give you full credit. You're at 'promising, under investigation.'" |
| 3. Flirtatious frame | Mutual discovery, not interview. | "You seem like trouble in the way that makes people make slightly worse but more memorable decisions." |
| 4. Personal reveal | A true thing about your taste or desire — vulnerable, not needy. | "I have a weakness for women who are warm, direct, and a little bit dangerous to my concentration." |
| 5. Romantic subtext | Name the charge lightly without grabbing her with it. | "This conversation is getting inconveniently distracting." |
| 6. Embodied implication | Bring physical, real-world possibility — still with restraint. | "I have a feeling this would be much more dangerous over a drink and eye contact." |
| 7. Direct escalation | When the signal is there, move. | "I like the charge here. Let's test it in person." |

Each level's reciprocation looks like one of: she teases back, escalates language, asks a personal question, makes a date-shaped hint, plays with the frame, gives more detail, responds quickly with warmth. See `references/profile-conversation-read.md` charge-mutuality signals.

### Four levers

1. **Specificity** — generic charge feels cheap. "It's the combination of softness and sharpness. That's the dangerous part" beats "you're sexy." Specificity makes desire feel earned.
2. **Restraint** — the explicit version is rarely the most charged version. "There is a lot I could say here, but I'm choosing maturity for at least one more message" creates more imagination than the explicit version. Restraint creates room to want.
3. **Mutuality** — without her energy back, you are broadcasting into a wall. Watch for the reciprocation signals above. If they are absent, do not climb the ladder; build warmth first.
4. **Pace** — sexual tension needs rhythm. `tease → warmth → desire → restraint → challenge → reward → subtext → normality`. Every message charged becomes try-hard. No message charged becomes platonic. The dial moves up and down across the thread.

### Notice → tease → reveal → imply → invite

The full charge build pattern across a thread, not within one message:

- **Notice**: warm specificity (Level 1).
- **Tease**: playful tension (Level 2).
- **Reveal**: personal reveal (Level 4).
- **Imply**: romantic subtext + embodied implication (Levels 5–6).
- **Invite**: direct escalation (Level 7), which is the conversion move below.

Within a single message you typically combine two adjacent steps, not all five.

### Charge calibration check

Before sending a Level 4+ charged draft, run silently:

- Has she earned escalation (reciprocated the previous level)?
- Is there warmth in the thread, not just engagement?
- Is this desire, or is this pressure dressed as desire?
- Does this leave her room to play, decline, or redirect?
- Would this feel exciting from her side, not merely expressive from the user's?
- Is `charge_level_attempted_max` (per `references/thread-state.md`) within `charge_level_reciprocated_max + 1`?

If any answer is no, drop one level or build warmth first.

### Working register: charged but not crude

Use as register, not boilerplate. Each carries desire under restraint:

- "There are several replies I could send here. I'm choosing the one that keeps us both pretending to be respectable." (restraint line)
- "You are not making it easy to maintain my calm, reasonable dating-app persona." (composure line)
- "I suspect this conversation would become significantly less innocent with eye contact." (eye-contact line)
- "You seem like someone who would be much more dangerous in person." (dangerous-in-person line)
- "I'm attracted to the energy here. Not just the profile. The rhythm." (controlled direct)
- "I'm a little more affected by this conversation than I planned to be." (small vulnerability)

### Vulnerability as charge

Small, specific, owned vulnerability is one of the strongest charge ingredients. The full structural rules live in the **Vulnerability framework** section below.

- Good: "I pretend to be rational, but warmth and good questions undo me pretty quickly."
- Bad: "I'm scared of liking someone and getting hurt again." (asks her to manage the user's fear; not charge)

The good form gives her a doorway. The bad form hands her an obligation.

### Shared implication

Give her a role inside the tension rather than declaring attraction at her. Statements that invite her to play with the frame:

- "I'm trying to work out whether you're actually dangerous or just well-branded."
- "You seem like the kind of woman who knows exactly when she's being distracting."

If she steps in ("definitely actually dangerous" / "maybe"), the next move acknowledges the step ("good. I was worried you were just decorative trouble" / "that was not a denial. Noted."). She is now helping build the charge with the user, not receiving it from the user.

## Vulnerability framework

Vulnerability is one of the most efficient charge ingredients when used correctly and one of the most repulsive when misused. The distinction is structural, not stylistic.

**Master distinction**: attractive vulnerability is *"I am real, and I am responsible for myself"*. Unattractive vulnerability is *"I am real, and now you are responsible for me"*. Drafts that fail this distinction land as obligation, not invitation, regardless of how honest they are.

**Formula**:

```text
truth + ownership + proportion + invitation = attractive vulnerability
```

- **Truth**: a real thing, not a curated reveal designed to look vulnerable.
- **Ownership**: held by the user, not handed to her to manage.
- **Proportion**: fits the stage of the thread; does not exceed earned depth.
- **Invitation**: leaves a doorway for her to step in, not a duty to absorb.

Anti-patterns and structural failure modes live in `references/boundaries.md` Vulnerability anti-patterns.

### Vulnerability ladder

Five levels. Move up only as the thread earns it; skipping levels is the most common vulnerability failure.

| Level | Type | What it reveals | Earliest landable stage |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Taste | What the user is drawn to, finds funny, can't stop noticing. | Aesthetic and attraction shape. | Always. Profile and openers. |
| 2. Values | What the user organises life around. | Operating frame. | Early chat. |
| 3. Desire | What the user wants from this kind of connection, including romantic and erotic register. | Direction of interest. | Mid-thread, once mutual interest is clear. |
| 4. Pattern awareness | What the user knows about himself, including failure modes. | Self-knowledge under restraint. | Mid-to-late thread or first date. |
| 5. Wounds | Past hurt, relational damage, ongoing struggle. | Real history. | Date stage or later, and only when reciprocated. |

Working examples by level:
- **Taste (1)**: "I have a weakness for women who are warm, direct, and a little bit dangerous to my concentration."
- **Values (2)**: "I'm not built for relationships that try to be everything to everyone."
- **Desire (3)**: "When I actually like someone I move pretty directly. I don't have a 'play it cool' phase."
- **Pattern awareness (4)**: "I'm pretty good at making the early stage feel romantic and pretty bad at being patient if it doesn't turn into something real."
- **Wounds (5)**: anything mapping here is almost always premature in chat. Default to inspection: keep it for in-person.

Stay one level above the level she has occupied unless the artifact clearly invites more.

### Calibration question

Before sending any vulnerable line, run silently:

> Am I revealing this because it serves the connection, or because I want reassurance?

If the answer is reassurance, the line is unowned. Rewrite into a smaller, owned version, or drop the reveal.

### Funnel-stage mapping

| Stage | Vulnerability ceiling | Form |
|---|---|---|
| Profile copy | 2 | Taste and values inside the polarising filter line. |
| Opener / early chat | 2-3 | Taste; values when relevant; light desire as observation. |
| Qualification phase | 3 | Desire as preference; small pattern reveal if the moment warrants. |
| Date transition | 3-4 | Direct desire; pattern awareness only if it adds charge to the invite. |
| Post first date | 4-5 | Pattern awareness; wounds only when reciprocated. |

The ceiling is the maximum landable level, not the target. Most messages should sit one or two levels below the ceiling — vulnerability that arrives every message becomes the user's identity rather than a moment.

## Conversion move

When there is rhythm, warmth, and at least one real revelation each side, stop chatting. The conversion move is short, direct, not over-explained, not softened into mush.

Working forms:
- "I like the rhythm here. Want to test the chemistry over a drink this week?"
- "This has enough signal that I'd rather not leave it trapped in app limbo. Want to continue it over a drink?"
- "I'm enjoying this enough that the app is starting to feel like the least interesting place for it. Drink this week?"

Anti-forms (rewrite before sending):
- "Wow you seem amazing, I would love to take you out sometime if you're interested" — auditioning, hedged.
- "We should hang out sometime" — vague, no logistical handle.
- "Would you maybe possibly want to grab coffee, no pressure" — apologising for the invitation.

The conversion move is gated by `escalation_readiness.ready === true` per `references/decision-rubric.md`.
